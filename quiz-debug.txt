1. Question? What does Ohm's Law state?
A. The resistance is directly proportional to the voltage.
B. The current is inversely proportional to the resistance.
C. The potential difference across a conductor is equal to its resistance.
D. The power dissipated in a resistor is equal to the product of voltage and current.

Answer: D
Explanation: Ohm's Law states that the power dissipated in a resistor is equal to the product of voltage and current, which is V = IR.

2. Question? What happens if the resistance remains constant while increasing the voltage?
A. The current increases.
B. The current decreases.
C. The current remains unchanged.
D. The potential difference decreases.

Answer: A
Explanation: According to Ohm's Law (V = IR), when the resistance is constant, an increase in voltage results in an increase in current if no other factors change.

3. Question? What does increasing the resistance do for a given voltage?
A. Decreases the current.
B. Increases the current.
C. Has no effect on the current.
D. The relationship between current and resistance cannot be determined without additional information.

Answer: A
Explanation: Increasing the resistance decreases the current, as stated in Ohm's Law (I = V/R).

4. Question? What is the unit of resistance?
A. Coulomb (C)
B. Ampere (A)
C. Volt (V)
D. Ohm (Ω)

Answer: D
Explanation: The unit of resistance is Ohm (Ω), which represents the ratio of voltage to current.

5. Question? Which statement correctly describes a valid application of Ohm's Law?
A. A 10 Ω resistor connected to a 20 V battery carries a current of 2 A.
B. Increasing the temperature increases the resistance in a conductor.
C. The potential difference across a conductor is directly proportional to its length.
D. The power dissipated in a resistor is independent of its resistance.

Answer: A
Explanation: According to Ohm's Law (P = IV), when the current remains constant, an increase in voltage results in an increase in power dissipation if no other factors change.
